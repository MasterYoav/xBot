A crew of AI agents on your Mac, run from anywhere

Projects, chats, notes and a whole company of agents, built around the agent CLIs you already pay for. Your iPhone and iPad drive it from wherever you are.

Build it from source

Free and open source. macOS 26 on Apple silicon, and at least one of Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity or GitHub Copilot. xBot 2.0 has no signed download yet; the 1.0 release is the old Docker app.

What it is

xBot is a native Mac app for working with AI agents. Add any folder as a project and talk to an agent about it in chats that sit as tabs in the title bar. Replies stream in as they are written, each tool the agent uses folds into one line, and in Plan mode the agent proposes steps you can edit before it runs them one by one. Beside the chat are the project's git changes, its pull request and checks, and its files. Each chat has its own terminal. Notes are Markdown in the project folder, and Automations run agent steps on a schedule, even while xBot is closed.

There is no model inside xBot and no server behind it. It runs the CLIs you already have — Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity and GitHub Copilot — one turn at a time, on your own subscriptions, with their own sign-in and permissions.

A crew, organised like a company

Agents are personas with a name, a role, instructions and a pixel-art look, each working through one of your CLIs. HeadMaster is the CEO and reports to you; a CTO or CFO reports to HeadMaster; engineers report to the CTO, and so on. You decide who reports to whom, and xBot suggests what most companies do. Managers hand work to their team, and finished work reports back up.

On the Agents page the crew lives in a little world — a village, a sky realm, an underwater kingdom, a moonbase or an enchanted forest — where they stroll, sit down at a desk to work, and say what they are doing.

Your Mac, in your pocket

The iPhone and iPad app is a remote for your Mac. Pair it with a QR code, and it reaches the Mac directly on the same network or, away from home, through your own iCloud, with every message sealed by the pairing key. Chats, projects and files, notes, automations, the crew and their world, abilities, a real terminal and your usage: all of it, in Liquid Glass.

Where your things live

  • Projects, chats, the crew and automations are kept in one file on your Mac. Notes live in the project folder.
  • What leaves the Mac is what you send to the agent you picked, on your own account, exactly as if you had typed it into its CLI.
  • xBot reads no passwords or sign-in tokens. Pairing keys are kept in the Keychain on the Mac and the phone, and nowhere else.
  • An agent on Full access can do what you can. xBot passes each CLI its own permission flags and says plainly what each mode allows.

Not built yet: an agent loop for people with an API key or Ollama instead of a CLI, and a computer of its own for an agent — a Linux VM with a browser you can watch and take over. The roadmap says what is next.

Removing it

Drag xBot to the Trash. To remove its library, settings, pairing keys and the automations helper as well, open Uninstall xBot.command from the disk image, or from the repository. It also clears anything xBot 1.x left behind.
